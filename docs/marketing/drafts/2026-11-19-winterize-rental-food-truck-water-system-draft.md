A winter rental can end on a mild Sunday afternoon. The next renter may not pick the truck up until Thursday, and the coldest night of the week falls in between. If no one has said who drains the water system during that gap, a fitting can split, a pump housing can crack or a water heater can be damaged while the truck sits locked in a lot.

This guide covers one narrow job: **protecting a rental food truck's or trailer's water system from freezing between bookings**, and getting it safely back into service. It covers tanks, hoses, pumps, strainers, the water heater, sink fixtures, lines and ice machines. It explains who should do what, what to write down, and why the equipment manuals decide the steps.

It builds on two earlier guides. Our [winter rental cash-flow guide](https://vendibook.com/blog/winter-food-truck-rental-off-season-cash-flow-2026) covers off-season pricing and demand. Our [rental maintenance inspection schedule](https://vendibook.com/blog/food-truck-rental-maintenance-inspection-checklist-2026) covers handoff checks for every system.

## Why freezing is a rental problem, not just a storage problem

The physics is simple. The National Weather Service's [cold-weather recovery guidance](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-after) puts it plainly: "Water expands as it freezes, causing pipes to burst." On a food truck, water sits in places that cool quickly: tanks under the floor, hoses and valves outside the body, a pump near the frame, and lines run along thin exterior walls.

Rentals add a people problem. An owner who stores the truck all winter usually winterizes it once, in a planned way. A rental unit may go back and forth between operating and parked several times a week. Each handoff is a moment when everyone can assume the other person dealt with the water. Most freeze losses on rental units come from that assumption, not from a lack of knowledge.

## Three cold situations need three different plans

### Seasonal storage

The unit will sit unused for weeks or months. This is when a full manufacturer-specified winterization makes the most sense: tanks and lines emptied, equipment drained or isolated as its manual directs, and everything recorded. Because the truck will be out of service for a while, it also needs a full return-to-service procedure before the next booking.

### Overnight cold snaps between short rentals

The unit is between bookings for a night or a few days, and a freeze is forecast. Fully winterizing and then dewinterizing it for each short gap takes time, and some steps may affect how the system must be flushed and sanitized before use. Options include keeping the unit in a heated space, using heating equipment the manufacturer approves, or draining the parts the manuals say are at risk. Which option is right depends on the equipment and the forecast. Agree in writing who decides and who acts.

### Active service in cold weather

The renter is operating, and the water system has to work. Monroe County, New York's [mobile food truck guidelines](https://www.monroecounty.gov/files/health/eh/food/Guidelines%20for%20Mobile%20Food%20Service%20Truck-Trailer.pdf) (updated March 2026) say that "Water systems must remain functional when operating facility" and that "Food trucks operated in winter will need heat." That is one county's rule, but the principle is common: a truck with frozen handwashing water is not ready to serve. Draining a system "just in case" during service is not an option, so the plan for operating days is about heat, monitoring and knowing when to stop.

## What can freeze on a food truck

Every unit is built differently, but these are the parts to ask the manufacturer or builder about:

- **Fresh and gray water tanks**, especially when mounted under the floor or outside the heated space.
- **Exposed hoses, valves and fill inlets**, including the hose used to fill the tank.
- **The water pump and its strainer or filter**, which can hold water after the tanks are empty.
- **The water heater**, which holds water and has its own drain and safety components.
- **Sink faucets, mixing valves and handwash fixtures**, and the short lines behind them.
- **Lines inside exterior walls or cabinets**, and any low points where water collects.
- **Ice machines and beverage equipment**, which have their own water paths and limits.

Ice machines show why there is no single rule. Ice-O-Matic's [ICE Series installation manual](https://iceomatic.com/content/store-documents/ICE_60Hz_449_Installation%20Manual.pdf) lists a minimum operating temperature of 50°F (10°C) and states that its icemakers "are not approved for outdoor installation." It warns that not following its installation guidelines "may void all warranties." That limit is for those models only, but it shows that an ice machine can have tighter limits than the plumbing around it.

## Your manual decides, not a universal number

There is no universal temperature at which every food truck must be winterized, no universal air pressure for clearing lines, and no single product that suits every system. Even within one company's products, the instructions differ.

- **Winnebago's 2026 towables owner's manual.** The [2026 Winnebago Towables Owner's Manual](https://www.winnebago.com/Files/Files/Winnebago/Resources/OperatorManuals/Operator%202026/2026%20-%20Winnebago%20Towables%20-%20Owner_s%20Manual%20%28Bound%20Documents%29.pdf) tells owners of those travel trailers to winterize "if the weather will be at or below 32 degrees Fahrenheit." Its procedure uses that unit's own drain points, a water heater bypass, a specified air pressure and RV antifreeze. It also says to sanitize the fresh water system "at least once a year or after taking the RV out of storage/winterization."
- **Two water heaters, two different steps.** A [Winnebago owner article on Truma water heaters](https://www.winnebago.com/lifestyle/winnebagolife/gear/truma-rv-water-heater-maintenance-winterization) (October 2025) says the temperature that calls for winterizing "depends on the unit." It describes closing the drain lever on one model after draining, but leaving the drain valve "open for the entire winter season" on another. It tells readers to check the operator's manual because steps vary by model and model year.

These are RV examples, used here only to show that procedures are specific to each manufacturer. **They are not instructions for a commercial mobile kitchen.** A food truck's water system supplies food preparation, warewashing and handwashing. The FDA's [Food Code 2022](https://www.fda.gov/food/fda-food-code/food-code-2022) is a model that state and local governments can adopt. It is not a federal mandate on its own, but many local codes are based on it. Under the [Food Code's water and plumbing chapter](https://www.fda.gov/files/2024-12-20-fodecode2022-print-0743.pdf), hoses carrying drinking water must be "safe" and finished with a "SMOOTH interior surface." Tanks, pumps and hoses for drinking water must be "used for no other purpose." Massachusetts' [mobile food establishment standards](https://www.mass.gov/guidance/retail-food-code-standards-for-mobile-food-establishments) require the water system and hoses to be "constructed with approved food-contact materials."

So before anyone puts antifreeze, compressed air or any chemical into a food truck's water system, confirm three things. The equipment manufacturer must allow it for that exact equipment. The product must be compatible with food-contact surfaces. And your local health authority must accept the method and the return-to-service steps. If any of the three is unclear, don't do it. Water heaters deserve extra caution: draining, bypassing or changing anything around their pressure and safety parts should follow the heater's manual or be left to a qualified technician.

## Who does what in a rental

Write the split into the rental agreement. Our guide to [food truck rental agreement clauses](https://vendibook.com/blog/food-truck-rental-agreement-contract-clauses-2026) explains how to word responsibility and damage terms. A common split, which your contract should confirm or change:

| Task | Usually the owner | Usually the renter |
|---|---|---|
| Know the manufacturer procedures and keep the manuals in the unit | Yes | Reads them before cold-weather use |
| Seasonal winterization and dewinterization | Yes, or the owner's technician | No, unless the agreement says so |
| Protection during a rental (heat, monitoring, stopping when needed) | Explains the setup at pickup | Yes, while the unit is in their care |
| Drain-down or protection at return when a freeze is forecast | Agrees in advance who does it | Does it only if trained and assigned in writing |
| Flushing, sanitizing and checking before the next booking | Yes | Confirms at pickup |
| Reporting suspected freeze damage | Arranges the technician | Reports the same day and stops using the system |

The handoff is the key moment. If a renter returns the unit on a day with a freeze forecast, the agreement should name the person who protects the water system that day, and that person should sign the return checklist.

## Document every winterization and dewinterization

A short written log protects both sides and the next renter. For each event, record:

- Date, time and who did it, including the technician's name if one was used.
- The procedure followed (manual title and section, or technician's work order).
- What was drained, isolated, heated or left running, and any product added.
- The forecast low that prompted it, and where the unit was parked.
- Photos of open drains, bypass settings and the control panel.
- For dewinterization: when the system was refilled from an approved source, flushed and sanitized, tested for leaks, and checked for hot water at the handwash sink.

Keep the log in the unit and in a shared folder the next renter can see before pickup.

## Bringing the water system back into service

Dewinterizing is not finished when water comes out of the tap. The Food Code's model rule says that "A water tank, pump, and hoses shall be flushed and SANITIZED before being placed in service after construction, repair, modification, and periods of nonuse." It also says a mobile water tank should be sloped to an outlet "that allows complete drainage of the tank," and that inlets, outlets and hoses must be capped or otherwise protected when not in use.

Massachusetts gives a practical example of what an inspector may check. Water must come "from an approved public water source or an approved well water source." Handwashing sinks need potable water at a minimum of 100°F. Wastewater must be removed at an approved servicing area or by a sewage transport vehicle, and may not be dumped "onto the ground surface, into waterways, or into storm drains." Draining a gray water tank before a freeze is still wastewater disposal, so it must go to an approved place.

Follow the sanitizing method and contact time your health authority accepts for food establishments, and the cleaning instructions for each piece of equipment. Don't assume an RV sanitizing method transfers.

## If you suspect freeze damage

Signs include no water flow, a pump that runs without delivering water, new drips or puddles, a cracked housing, ice in a line, or a water heater that won't light or shows an error. If you see any of them:

1. **Stop using the water system and stop food service** if handwashing or warewashing water is affected.
2. **Turn off the pump and the water heater** using the controls described in their manuals. Don't keep trying to start them.
3. **Don't use an open flame or improvised heat.** The National Weather Service says to "Never use an open flame" when warming frozen pipes. Don't heat lines, tanks or valves yourself.
4. **Call a qualified technician** to inspect the system before anyone runs water again. The Food Code says a plumbing system shall be "Repaired according to LAW" and "Maintained in good repair."
5. **Document and notify.** Take photos, record times, and tell the other party the same day. Ask your health authority whether repairs or a return to service need to be reported or re-inspected.

## An illustrative owner loss

A rental ends on Sunday. The return checklist doesn't say who protects the water system, and nobody does. A hard freeze arrives Monday night. On Tuesday, the owner finds a split fitting behind the handwash sink and a cracked pump housing.

| Cost item | Illustrative amount |
|---|---|
| Technician diagnosis, pump and fitting replacement | $480 |
| Drying and repairing a water-damaged cabinet base | $350 |
| Two booked days cancelled at $275 per day | $550 |
| Flushing, sanitizing and leak testing before reopening | $150 |
| **Total illustrative loss** | **$1,530** |

The prevention side of this example is about one hour: a forecast check, a named person on the return checklist, the manufacturer procedure, and a log entry. The exact numbers will differ for every unit. What usually doesn't change is that the costly part is the lost bookings and water damage, not the drain-down itself.

## Practical checklist

**Before winter (owner)**
- [ ] Collect the manuals for the water heater, pump, ice machine and any other water-using equipment.
- [ ] Ask the builder or a technician which parts are at freeze risk and how each should be protected.
- [ ] Confirm with your health authority which flushing and sanitizing steps it expects before reopening.
- [ ] Add winterization, protection-at-return and freeze-damage terms to the rental agreement.

**At every winter handoff (owner and renter together)**
- [ ] Check the forecast low for the days the unit will sit.
- [ ] Name who protects the water system, and how, on the signed checklist.
- [ ] Photograph drains, bypass settings and control panels.

**Before the next booking (owner)**
- [ ] Refill from an approved source, flush, sanitize, check for leaks and confirm hot handwashing water.
- [ ] Update the log and share it with the renter.

## FAQ

**At what temperature should I winterize a food truck?**
There isn't one universal number. Winnebago's towables manual uses 32°F for those trailers, a Truma article says the trigger depends on the water heater model, and one commercial ice machine manual lists 50°F as its minimum operating temperature. Use the limits in your own equipment's manuals.

**Can I use RV antifreeze in a food truck?**
Only if the equipment manufacturer allows it for that equipment, the product is suitable for food-contact surfaces, and your health authority accepts it along with your return-to-service steps. RV instructions don't automatically apply to a commercial mobile kitchen.

**Who pays if the lines freeze between rentals?**
Whatever the agreement says. Write down who protects the system at return and who pays for damage when that step is skipped.

**Do I need to sanitize after winterizing?**
The Food Code model rule calls for flushing and sanitizing tanks, pumps and hoses after periods of nonuse and after repairs. Your local code may say more.

**Can the renter keep serving if water is still flowing?**
If you suspect any freeze damage, stop and have it inspected. Small leaks can get worse quickly and can contaminate the water supply.

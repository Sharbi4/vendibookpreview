## Truck purchase diligence and trailer transport

Topic: vehicle_and_towing. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- Tow capacity and payload are different limits; passengers, cargo, hitch equipment and trailer tongue load affect the tow vehicle. Use the exact vehicle configuration and manufacturer documentation, not the model name or a marketing maximum.
- A newer kitchen installed on an older truck does not make the chassis new. Odometer readings should be compared with maintenance/inspection records and title information.

### Discovery questions

- What are the chassis year, kitchen build year, VIN/title status, odometer and known defects?
- What equipment is included, financed, leased or excluded from the sale?
- What is the trailer’s actual loaded weight with water, fuel, inventory and equipment?
- What tow vehicle configuration, payload/axle ratings, hitch limits and brake setup are documented?

### Suggested next steps

- Arrange qualified mechanical and kitchen inspections and verify title/ownership documentation through the appropriate channels before committing.
- Have a qualified provider assess the complete loaded towing combination, tires, brakes, lights, frame and hitch. Check route dimensions and applicable licensing requirements.
- Ask for current recall/history results without claiming Atlas ran a VIN lookup. A history report does not certify the kitchen or replace inspection.
- Use an approved seller-message draft to resolve missing facts. Keep formal purchase terms and handoff confirmation in the buyer-controlled workflow.

### Limits

- Do not certify towing safety from empty weight, trailer length or a photo.
- Manufacturer guidance for Ford does not establish another manufacturer’s limits; retrieve the actual manual.
- Do not infer clear title, lien release or roadworthiness from a listing badge or seller assurance.

### Sources

- [Ford](https://www.ford.com/towing/) — Manufacturer towing/payload guidance; actual model year and configuration control.
- [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/odometer-fraud) — Used-vehicle odometer and record checks; not a comprehensive inspection.
- [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) — Official recall lookup; Atlas must not claim a lookup without a result.

## Repeatable operating habits and launch review

Topic: operating_routine. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- Operational consistency depends on the actual crew, layout and procedures. CDC restaurant research supports accessible handwashing facilities, training and management attention to handwashing.
- A launch checklist is an editorial planning aid, not a food-safety certification or substitute for locally required training.

### Discovery questions

- Who owns opening, receiving, prep, service, cleaning and closing tasks?
- Can staff wash hands when needed without blocked access or competing tasks?
- How will an ill worker report symptoms and be replaced under the applicable employee-health rules?
- What is the response to loss of water, power, refrigeration, unsafe food or equipment failure?

### Suggested next steps

- Before launch: verify permission and approvals, equipment readiness, supplier/receiving arrangements, training and the actual menu workflow.
- During a permitted trial: measure order-to-handoff time, stockouts, waste, customer feedback and contribution by channel. Record problems rather than expanding immediately.
- Review weekly: adjust one bottleneck, schedule preventive maintenance and reassess staffing, hours and menu complexity using observed results.
- Maintain required safety records and follow the authority’s procedures for illness, temperature excursions and reopening after a failure. Do not sell food whose safety cannot be established.

### Limits

- No generic launch deadline or guaranteed success from following a checklist.
- Do not invent required log frequencies, employee exclusion periods or reopening criteria; use the local authority’s rules.
- Sales speed never justifies skipping food-safety controls.

### Sources

- [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/restaurant-food-safety/php/practices/handwashing.html) — Restaurant handwashing research and management practices; not all local operating rules.
- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code) — Model Food Code; adoption and amendments vary by jurisdiction.

## A renter’s delivery-only kitchen pathway

Topic: delivery_kitchen. Review interval: 30 days.

### Reference summary

- A delivery-only operation may be a useful business model from an approved rented kitchen. A virtual brand is the customer-facing concept; the kitchen is its production location. Renting equipment alone does not authorize food sales or establish platform eligibility.
- DoorDash virtual brands must operate from a licensed commercial kitchen, not a residential cottage-food kitchen. Its reviewed rules include differentiated main menus, authentic item photos and operating-quality criteria. General merchant FAQs and virtual-brand-specific rules are different scopes.
- Uber’s virtual-restaurant help describes a separate concept within an existing physical store, using a kitchen licensed to sell food. Obtain platform confirmation for the actual shared-kitchen or mobile-unit arrangement; do not infer that every rented truck qualifies.

### Discovery questions

- What city and actual operating address will you use?
- Does the host allow your business to prepare food and dispatch delivery drivers there?
- Is the operator licensed for this menu, process and address?
- Are the rented hours, storage, parking and driver pickup access sufficient?

### Suggested next steps

- Offer naturally: “A delivery-only brand could be another way to use a rented kitchen. Want to check whether the space and your menu are a fit?”
- Confirm the rental agreement, regulator requirements and platform eligibility before committing to a launch. Search actual kitchen listings using ghost_kitchen; do not substitute a truck without permission.
- Prepare an accurate menu, real food photos, operating hours, pickup instructions and order-handling process. The operator completes platform onboarding and any business/banking verification directly; never collect credentials or bank details in Atlas.
- Run a small permitted pilot with packaging and travel tests. Review orders, contribution after costs, preparation delays, complaints and repeat business before expanding.

### Limits

- No automatic activation, guaranteed visibility, order volume or profit.
- No unapproved relocation of a platform address; confirm changes with the platform and regulator.
- Do not present generated menu-concept images as authentic delivery-platform food photography.

### Sources

- [DoorDash](https://help.doordash.com/en-us/merchants/article/official-doordash-virtual-brand-quality-requirements) — US virtual-brand eligibility and ongoing quality rules; verify before launch.
- [DoorDash](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/faq) — General merchant onboarding, hours and order handling.
- [Uber](https://help.uber.com/en/merchants-and-restaurants/article/creating-a-virtual-restaurant?nodeId=15dac416-35d3-47a0-940b-2e465a404ac9) — Virtual-restaurant program; not blanket approval for mobile kitchens.
- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-industry/how-start-food-business) — US food business regulatory overview; local requirements depend on operation.

## Electrical capacity and generator discovery

Topic: electrical. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- Generator running output and temporary starting output are different. Motor loads can need additional starting power. A list of appliance names or a generator’s peak rating is insufficient to confirm compatibility.
- Manufacturer manuals and nameplates must establish each load’s voltage, phase, current/power and startup demand. A matching plug shape or total wattage alone does not establish a compliant installation.

### Discovery questions

- Which exact appliances, refrigeration, HVAC, water heater, pumps and lights run together?
- Can the seller provide equipment nameplates, generator model/manual and panel/circuit documentation?
- Will the site supply shore power, generator power or both, and what service is actually available?
- Has a qualified electrician verified circuits, per-leg loading, protection, transfer arrangements and the complete installation?

### Suggested next steps

- Build an equipment schedule with model, supply requirements, running/start demand, intended simultaneous use and evidence still missing.
- Have a qualified electrician evaluate the installed system against manufacturer instructions and locally adopted electrical rules, including wiring, inlet, connectors, grounding/bonding and GFCI protection.
- Keep preliminary load arithmetic labeled as an estimate, not a generator recommendation or permission to energize. Include future additions and operating conditions in the professional assessment.

### Limits

- Do not prescribe wire/breaker sizes, bonding changes, bypasses, adapters or DIY transfer connections.
- Never backfeed a building or trailer through an improvised connection. Never claim a universal amperage or generator size fits food trucks.
- Do not treat an older OSHA construction-oriented fact sheet as the complete code for every mobile kitchen.

### Sources

- [Honda Power Equipment](https://powerequipment.honda.com/generators/generator-how-much-power) — Manufacturer explanation of running and starting loads; illustrative examples are not truck-specific specifications.
- [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/GROUNDING_PORT_GENERATOR.pdf) — Portable-generator electrical hazards and qualified installation guidance; scope and current applicable rules need verification.

## Fire, fuel and carbon-monoxide readiness

Topic: fire_and_fuel. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- Cooking method and fuel influence fire-protection needs. A hood visible in a photo does not prove suppression coverage, inspection or approval.
- Portable engine generators produce poisonous carbon monoxide. Operate outdoors away from openings and air intakes, according to manufacturer and authority requirements; never inside the truck or an enclosed space.

### Discovery questions

- Does the menu produce grease-laden vapors, involve deep frying or use solid fuel?
- What hood/suppression service records and local fire inspection evidence are available?
- What fuel system, cylinder mounting and generator placement are installed, and who verified them?
- Does the chosen site meet fire access, separation and event-specific requirements?

### Suggested next steps

- Ask the fire authority to confirm applicable rules for the actual unit, equipment and site.
- Request professional inspection and records rather than advising a buyer to test or alter gas systems.
- Plan staff training, emergency shutoffs, appropriate extinguishers, maintenance and safe refueling procedures.

### Limits

- NFPA is a standards organization, not the local government approving this site. Adopted editions and local requirements control.
- No remote fire approval, leak-testing instructions or universal clearance claim.
- For a suspected active gas leak, fire or CO exposure, stop the sales discussion: move to safety and contact emergency services; do not troubleshoot in the danger area.

### Sources

- [NFPA](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Code-or-topic-fact-sheets/FoodTruckFactSheet.pdf?rev=5cac27242cf445638f80ba05b78fa9c7) — 2024 mobile cooking checklist; referenced standards require local applicability review.
- [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA4105.pdf) — Portable generator carbon-monoxide safety.

## Local approvals, commissary and water planning

Topic: permits_and_water. Review interval: 60 days.

### Reference summary

- The operating address, menu, food process, vehicle type and sales channel determine which questions to ask regulators. A seller’s permit does not establish a new operator’s approval.
- Florida DBPR describes self-sufficient mobile units and commercial nonresidential water/wastewater servicing. This is Florida guidance, not a national rule; regulator exceptions and actual capacity matter.
- Texas has a state mobile-food licensing pathway. Local site, fire and other requirements must still be checked for the proposed operation.

### Discovery questions

- Where will you prepare, store, serve and dispose of waste, including the base/commissary?
- Is the address inside city limits, unincorporated county, an event or a special jurisdiction?
- Will you use raw ingredients, cooling/reheating, wholesale packaging or specialized processes?
- What approved potable-water source, hot water, sinks, wastewater service and storage capacity are documented?

### Suggested next steps

- Call get_permit_requirements with actual operating city/state and known operation. Keep returned coverage gaps and scope explicit.
- Prepare a menu/process description, equipment layout, site/address, water/waste plan and ownership/rental arrangement for agency review before purchase or alterations.
- Confirm health, fire, zoning/site permission, business/tax registration and vehicle/towing obligations with their respective authorities. A single approval does not substitute for the rest.

### Limits

- Never invent universal tank sizes, sink configurations, fees, approval times or permit transfer rules.
- The local library has selected markets only; unknown jurisdictions require new government research.
- FDA model guidance is not automatically the locally adopted rule.

### Sources

- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-industry/how-start-food-business) — US food business regulatory overview; local requirements depend on operation.
- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code) — Model Food Code; adoption and amendments vary by jurisdiction.
- [Florida DBPR](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/mfdv-guide/) — Florida MFDV guide and servicing requirements, with regulator exceptions.
- [Texas DSHS](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/retail-food-establishments/permits-retail-food-establishments/mobile-food-vendors) — Texas mobile food licensing; not a nationwide permit.

## Menu engineering and service flow

Topic: menu_and_service. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- A useful menu fits documented appliances, storage, staff and production capacity. Cuisine labels do not establish equipment fit.
- Delivery introduces packaging, temperature, contamination and allergen-control considerations beyond the cooking step. A product that travels poorly can create waste and complaints even when it tastes good at pickup.

### Discovery questions

- Which items use the same ingredients and preparation steps?
- What step limits service: cooking, assembly, payment, packaging or storage?
- How does each item hold up during the expected delivery trip?
- Can the operation prevent allergen cross-contact and accurately communicate ingredients without making unsupported allergen-free claims?

### Suggested next steps

- As a planning recommendation, pilot a focused menu that fits the real listing. Test prep time, portion yield, packaging, travel quality and waste with the actual crew.
- Map receiving through storage, prep, cook, hold, pack, handoff and cleaning; verify required food-safety controls with the responsible authority.
- Use show_menu_concept for supported listing equipment. Explain recipe ideas as possibilities, not a certified menu or an earnings forecast.

### Limits

- No invented cooking/holding temperatures, shelf lives, allergen guarantees or safety plans.
- Do not label simulated dishes as food already produced by this operator.

### Sources

- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-highlights-best-practices-food-safety-online-delivery-services) — Delivery food-safety guidance, not platform eligibility.
- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code) — Model Food Code; adoption and amendments vary by jurisdiction.

## Contribution, break-even and cash planning

Topic: economics. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- Break-even uses fixed cost divided by positive unit contribution. Gross sales and sales after food cost are not net profit.
- A useful planning comparison separates one-time setup costs, ongoing fixed costs and costs that change with each order. Use the operator’s own quotes, contracts and records.

### Discovery questions

- What remains from an order after ingredients, packaging, channel fees, operator-funded promotions and other variable costs?
- Which labor costs are incremental and which are scheduled fixed costs?
- What are rent/commissary, insurance, maintenance, software, utilities and financing cash commitments?
- How much cash is needed before payouts arrive, and what happens in a slow week or equipment outage?

### Suggested next steps

- Build separate in-person, catering and delivery scenarios using actual fee terms; do not apply a guessed platform commission.
- Compute contribution with clearly stated cost assumptions. If contribution is zero or negative, there is no positive finite break-even volume under those assumptions.
- For mixed menus, use the expected sales mix rather than treating every item as identical. Compare required orders with realistic production capacity and measured demand.
- Track cash flow separately from accounting profit; financing principal and equipment purchases affect cash differently from operating expenses. Seek an accountant for tax/accounting treatment.

### Limits

- No default industry margin, guaranteed revenue, loan approval or earnings promise.
- estimate_earnings only gives sales after food cost before other expenses; say that clearly.
- Seller-covered freight must remain Free shipping; never reveal internal freight costs.

### Sources

- [US Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) — Business planning and break-even framework; not food-truck profit benchmarks.

## Validate demand and choose selling channels

Topic: demand_and_channels. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- National industry counts do not establish demand for one menu at one location. Census mobile-food establishment counts are not counts of individual trucks and are tied to a dataset/year.
- Street vending, scheduled private-site service, events, catering and delivery have different operating constraints. Treat channel ideas as hypotheses to test, not guaranteed sales.

### Discovery questions

- Who is the intended customer and when do they buy?
- What alternatives, prices and waiting times can you actually observe nearby?
- What written site permission, event charges, cancellation rules or minimum commitments apply?
- Which channel can the current equipment and crew serve consistently?

### Suggested next steps

- Recommend an authorized small pilot: record day/time, weather, location, hours, inquiries, transactions, average ticket, contribution, waste and service time.
- Compare repeatable net contribution per operating hour, not only gross sales at the busiest event.
- Test one meaningful change at a time: menu, location, hours, packaging or offer. Keep a stable schedule where permitted and use accurate customer-facing hours.
- For catering, verify capacity, delivery/setup, service duration and written scope before quoting. Do not promise availability or terms on a host’s behalf.

### Limits

- No invented foot traffic, conversion rate, local rankings, success rate or market-size extrapolation.
- Do not use old pandemic growth data as a current forecast.
- Atlas does not enroll users in platforms or send promotional campaigns without the appropriate authorization and consent.

### Sources

- [US Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) — Business planning and break-even framework; not food-truck profit benchmarks.
- [US Census Bureau](https://data.census.gov/profile/722330_-_Mobile_food_services?codeset=naics~722330&g=010XX00US) — Mobile food services industry definition and dated establishment data; not vehicle inventory or local demand.

## Rental and shared-kitchen launch checklist

Topic: rental_launch. Review interval: 90 days.

### Reference summary

- Rental access, permission to operate a particular business and regulatory approval are separate issues.
- A daily listing price does not establish an hourly rate, recurring availability, storage rights or an all-inclusive membership.

### Discovery questions

- What production and cleanup hours are needed, and are equipment and storage exclusive or shared?
- Who handles cleaning, grease, trash, supplies, repairs and equipment downtime?
- Does the host allow your menu, brand, driver pickup, overnight storage and proposed operating address use?
- What documents, insurance, access rules, deposits and cancellation terms does the actual booking require?

### Suggested next steps

- Check real listing details and dates; ask the host about undocumented needs through the approved messaging flow.
- Before a delivery launch, review delivery_kitchen guidance and obtain required host, authority and platform confirmations.
- Document handoff condition and included equipment, read booking terms and plan a production trial before accepting customer commitments.
- Use get_faq for Vendibook payment, insurance, deposit and cancellation policy; do not replace current booking terms with this general checklist.

### Limits

- Do not promise permit transfer, insurance coverage, recurring slots or refunds.
- No automatic subletting or use of the host’s platform account or licenses.

### Sources

- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-industry/how-start-food-business) — US food business regulatory overview; local requirements depend on operation.
- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code) — Model Food Code; adoption and amendments vary by jurisdiction.

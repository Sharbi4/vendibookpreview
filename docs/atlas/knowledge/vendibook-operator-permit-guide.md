# Vendibook operator and permit guide

Knowledge base for every Vendibook agent: Atlas, Allison, and the phone line Allison on 866-690-6227.

This is reference research reviewed on **October 26, 2026**, summarized from the agencies and laws named in each section. It is not legal advice and not a permit approval.

Rules change. Every answer must name the agency to confirm with, and must never promise approval, a fee or a processing time. If a section says something "conflicts" or is "unconfirmed", say so.

## How agents use this guide

1. **Ask before advising.** Ask four things:
   - which city and county they'll operate in, and the exact site if they know it;
   - what they'll serve: packaged only, simple heat-and-serve, or full cooking;
   - what the unit is: truck, trailer, cart or shared kitchen;
   - whether they have a commissary or base kitchen.
2. **Give the short version first.** That's two or three steps and who issues the permit. Then offer more: "Would you like me to walk through the full checklist for your city?" or "Want to learn more about the fire inspection?" Always ask whether they'd like more detail when it would help, and respect a no.
3. **Name the source agency and say when this was reviewed.** For example: "As of our October 2026 review, Texas DSHS issues the license. Check their site for current fees."
4. **Offer to send links.** On the phone, use send_listing_links or a team follow-up. Never read long URLs aloud.
5. **For a place not covered here,** explain how it usually works (state or county health permit, commissary, fire, local business license, location rules). Say that you haven't verified that city's rules, and offer a team follow-up.

## The universal checklist

Almost every US jurisdiction asks for some version of these. Order matters: plan review comes before you build or buy changes.

| Step | What it is | Who usually issues it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business setup | Entity (LLC etc.), EIN, state sales-tax permit, local business license or business tax receipt | Secretary of State, IRS, state revenue department, city or county |
| Plan review | Drawings, equipment specs and menu approved **before** building, remodeling or re-licensing a unit | The health authority that will permit the unit |
| Health permit or license | A permit for the unit, often tied to its menu or risk level | State health or agriculture department, or the county or local health department, depending on state |
| Commissary or base of operation | A permitted kitchen used for water, wastewater, cleaning, storage and prep. Many states require a daily return. | Agreement with a permitted facility, filed with the health permit |
| Food safety certification | A certified food protection manager, and food handler cards for staff where required | Accredited programs (ANSI) |
| Fire inspection | Hood and suppression, extinguishers, propane, generator | Local fire marshal or fire department, often yearly or every 6 months |
| Vehicle | Title, registration and plates, commercial auto insurance; a CDL above federal weight thresholds | State DMV. FMCSA rules for CDL |
| Where you can sell | Zoning, written permission from private property owners, public right-of-way or street permits, event permits, distance and hours rules | City or county planning, parks, police or event organizers |
| Insurance | General liability, commercial auto, often naming the city or event as additional insured | Your insurer. Some cities set minimums, e.g. Chicago requires $350,000 per occurrence for propane or natural-gas trucks |

## Building and equipment standards (trucks and trailers)

These are the common inspection points. The exact rules come from the editions of the Food Code, NFPA 96, NFPA 1 and NFPA 58 that a jurisdiction has adopted, so always confirm with the local health department and fire marshal.

- **Food equipment:** commercial (often NSF/ANSI-certified) equipment.
  - A separate handwash sink with hot and cold water.
  - A three-compartment sink for warewashing, unless the commissary handles it.
  - Adequate refrigeration and hot holding.
  - Smooth, cleanable surfaces.
- **Water:** potable freshwater tanks and wastewater tanks sized to the jurisdiction's rules.
  - Many jurisdictions require the wastewater tank to be larger than the freshwater tank. Confirm the required sizes.
  - Wastewater goes to an approved disposal point, usually the commissary.
- **Cooking with grease vapors (fryers, griddles):**
  - A Type I hood with an automatic fire-suppression system, inspected and serviced on the local schedule. Six months is common, citing NFPA 96, but intervals vary by city.
  - A Class K extinguisher near fryers.
  - A multipurpose ABC extinguisher (often 2A:10BC or larger) on every unit.
  - Some fire codes require spacing between fryers and open flames.
- **Propane:** NFPA 1 Chapter 50 and NFPA 58 rules:
  - cylinder limits;
  - cylinders secured upright with protected valves;
  - LP-rated hose only;
  - leak checks, and venting for enclosed compartments.

  Several fire marshals ban copper tubing inside the vehicle.
- **Generators:** usually must be outside the cooking area, with exhaust away from the service window. Some cities require a fire permit for generators.
- **Plan review before build:** buying a used unit does not carry its approval with it. Remodels, a change of menu risk level, or moving to a new state or county often trigger a new plan review.
  - Example: Florida DBPR requires plan review for a vehicle never licensed by the division, one closed more than 18 months, or one that was remodeled.

## Buying or renting a used truck or trailer

Questions an agent should suggest before money changes hands:

- Is the title clear and in the seller's name? Does the VIN match the title?
- Is it currently permitted, where, and under which license type or menu? Can the buyer see the last health and fire inspection reports?
- Will the buyer's planned menu require a different license type, for example cooking instead of prepackaged?
- When were the hood and suppression system last serviced? Are the propane system and generator in good condition?
- What are the tank sizes, and is there hot water?
- What is its loaded weight and GVWR? Is the tow vehicle rated for it?
- Federal rules require a Class A CDL when the combined rating is 26,001 lb or more **and** the trailer rating is over 10,000 lb. Trailer brake thresholds vary by state; Virginia, for example, requires brakes at 3,000 lb actual gross weight.

## Shared and ghost kitchens

- Most jurisdictions license the **kitchen operator** and also require each **tenant food business** to hold its own permit.
- The tenant usually needs:
  - a written access or use agreement;
  - a copy of the operator's permit;
  - a menu and equipment list;
  - a sales-tax license;
  - an inspection that includes the tenant's assigned area.
- Examples:
  - **Allegheny County, PA:** a "shared kitchen" means 3 or more permit holders in one facility, and users need a Shared Kitchen User permit.
  - **Chicago:** separate Shared Kitchen and Shared Kitchen User licenses.
  - **New York City:** the operator needs a Non-Retail Food Processing Establishment permit.
  - **Long Beach, CA:** a tenant agreement and notice within 10 days if the contract ends.
  - **Orange County, CA:** may not need a separate dependent operator permit for short, specific access when the primary operator takes responsibility.
- A shared kitchen can also serve as a food truck's **commissary**, if the health department approves it for that and the agreement covers the services it provides: water, wastewater, cleaning and storage.
- A home kitchen is generally **not** accepted as a commissary. Some states allow home-based cottage food for low-risk foods, but it does not cover food trucks.

## State-by-state notes

These cover the states where Vendibook has the most listings, plus Minnesota. Local cities and counties still control where you can operate, and many also require a business license and fire inspection.

### Texas

- **What changed:** since **July 1, 2026**, Texas DSHS issues **one statewide mobile food vendor license** under HB 2844. It replaced city and county health permits for food trucks, pushcarts and roadside vendors.
- **License types by preparation:**
  - Type I: prepackaged, non-TCS foods.
  - Type II: limited preparation, cook-serve.
  - Type III: complex preparation, cooking, holding, cooling and reheating.
- **Fees:** sources conflict. DSHS's 2025 draft listed Type I $250, Type II $500 and Type III $800. A later city-hosted guide lists $309, $618 + $400 inspection, and $876 + $500 inspection. Tell callers to check the DSHS Mobile Food Vendors page for the current fee.
- **Inspection:** a pre-licensing inspection comes before the license is issued. The license expires one year after that inspection.
- **Public database:** a public database lists licensed vendors with inspection results.
- **What cities still control:** zoning, fire and life safety, traffic, noise and event permits. Lewisville, for example, says the state license doesn't exempt vendors from local location rules. Hays County says it is no longer the permitting authority.
- **Commissary or central preparation facility:** not covered in the sources reviewed. Confirm with DSHS.
- Sources: [DSHS Mobile Food Vendors](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/mobile-food-vendors), [DSHS HB 2844 overview](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/phfpcommittee/docs/phfpc-hb-2844-overview-10.08.2025.pdf), [Lewisville](https://www.cityoflewisville.com/city-hall/city-departments/health-services/mobile-vendors), [KSAT](https://ksat.com/news/local/2026/06/05/texas-opens-applications-for-new-statewide-food-truck-mobile-vendor-license-required-july-1/).

### Florida

- **State license:** Florida DBPR (Division of Hotels and Restaurants) licenses Mobile Food Dispensing Vehicles (MFDVs).
- **Preemption:** Section 509.102 preempts local licenses, registrations, permits and fees for MFDVs to the state, effective July 1, 2020. Localities can't ban them across their whole jurisdiction, but they can still regulate where and how they operate.
- **Locally:** a business tax receipt, a fire inspection, and location, signage and hours rules. Jacksonville is an example.
- **Application:** HR 7036, with a commissary form (HR 7022). Fees depend on application date and county.
- **Plan review:** needed if the vehicle was never licensed by the division, was closed more than 18 months, or was remodeled.
- **Self-sufficient vehicles:** sources disagree on whether a self-sufficient vehicle can skip a commissary. Check DBPR rule 61C-4.0161.
- **Pensacola:** an old city ordinance still mentions a city permit. Confirm locally.
- Sources: [DBPR MFDV guide](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/hotels-restaurants/licensing/mfdv-guide/), [s. 509.102](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/509.102), [Jacksonville](https://www.jacksonville.gov/departments/neighborhoods/office-of-consumer-affairs/food-trucks).

### California

- **Permit:** the county or city environmental health department permits Mobile Food Facilities, after a plan check under the California Retail Food Code. Example: [LA County plan check guidelines](https://publichealth.lacounty.gov/eh/inspection/plan-check-operational-guidelines-mobile-food-facilities-mobile-support-unit.htm).
- **SB 972 (from January 1, 2023):** created Compact Mobile Food Operations (CMFOs), small carts and stands. These have prepackaged and limited-preparation tiers, with county permits and possible plan check. See San Bernardino's [SB 972 sheet](https://www.sbcounty.gov/uploads/dph/ehs/Programs/Food/FoodFacilities/sb-972-informational-sheet.pdf).
- **Plan review timing:** counties must complete plan review within 20 working days ([Monterey](https://www.countyofmonterey.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/15054/638912119794730000)).
- **City rules:** sidewalk vending and city business licenses are separate. No 2026 statewide changes were found.

### Arizona

- **Reciprocity:** under existing law (A.R.S. § 36-1761), a mobile food unit license issued by one county health department is honored in every Arizona county.
- **2026 change that failed:** HB 2118 would have stopped cities, towns and counties from requiring a separate mobile vendor license, and capped repeat fire inspections. It was **vetoed** by the Governor in 2026, so local licenses and fire inspections can still apply.
- **Earlier guidance:** this repo's earlier Tucson and Pima County notes still apply.
- Sources: [HB 2118 veto summary](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/2R/summary/H.HB2118_070826_VETOED.DOCX.htm).

### Georgia

- **Base-county permit:** since January 1, 2023, the permit comes from the county of your **base of operation**, and other counties recognize it (HB 1443).
- **Other counties:** to operate elsewhere, submit authorization-to-operate paperwork to each additional county's environmental health office.
- **Base rules:** the base must be a fixed, permitted food service location. It can't be shared with another permit holder's base, and a restaurant's own permit can't serve as the truck's base permit.
- **Location changes:** the DPH application form (rev. 06/2026) asks for 7 days' notice of changes in vending locations.
- **Rules:** Chapter 511-6-1.
- Sources: [DPH mobile food FAQ](https://dph.georgia.gov/document/document/mobile-food-frequency-asked-questions/download), [DPH application](https://dph.georgia.gov/document/document/dph-mobile-food-unit-permit-application-pdf/download).

### Tennessee

- **State permit:** the Tennessee Department of **Health** permits mobile food units under its food service establishment rules.
- **City permits:** many cities add their own. Knoxville's mobile food unit permit is $200 a year, and Chattanooga requires fire, county health and state certifications.
- **Ordinances vary:** some cities have no food-truck ordinance at all.
- Sources: [Knoxville](https://www.knoxvilletn.gov/mobilefood), [MTAS ordinance review](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/mrln/mknowledge/main/MTAS%20Food%20Truck%20Ord%20Review_2021.pdf).

### New Jersey

- **Base rules:** state retail food rules (N.J.A.C. 8:24) are the baseline, but licensing is **municipal**. Expect a local health license, a mercantile or business license, a fire permit, and sometimes zoning or police sign-off.
- **Municipal examples:**
  - Passaic caps licenses and has a designated "Food Truck Row".
  - Rockaway and Randolph license trucks for approved events only.
  - Some towns set hours or distance limits.
- No statewide guide was found. Check the specific town.

### North Carolina

- **Rules:** 15A NCAC 18A .2600.
- **Permit:** issued by the county that inspects the unit's **commissary**. The unit reports to the commissary daily, as county guidance cites .2670(d).
- **Application:** counties ask for a menu, operating times and locations, a signed commissary agreement, and a plan review fee (Guilford: $200).
- **Disclosure:** operators must list every county and location they'll work in.
- **Commissary:** private homes can't be commissaries.
- Source: [Guilford County](https://guilfordcountync.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/7118/636846311849670000), [Harnett County](https://www.harnett.org/health/mobile-food-unit-and-pushcart-requirements.asp).

### Virginia

- **Permit:** VDH mobile food unit permits are issued through local health districts as a sticker, renewed yearly.
- **No statewide reciprocity was found.** Plan on one permit per health district.
- **Local vendor permits:** cities and counties (Richmond, Norfolk, Fairfax and others) add their own vendor permits and site rules.
- Sources: [Loudoun](https://www.loudoun.gov/5293/Mobile-Food-Unit), [VDH notice](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/content/uploads/sites/20/2016/04/Upcoming-Changes-for-Mobile-Food-Units_Final.pdf).

### South Carolina

- **Agency:** retail food permitting now uses **SC Department of Agriculture** forms, under Regulation 61-25.
- **Permits:** a mobile food establishment is a commissary plus its units. **Both** need permits, and so do pushcarts.
- **Commissary:** units return to the commissary after each day of operation and are stored there. Home-based commissaries must also clear local zoning.
- **Paperwork:** the 2026 Mobile Food Establishment Supplemental form (CPD #2206) documents what the commissary provides.
- Sources: [SCDA 2026 supplemental](https://agriculture.sc.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/MobileFoodEstablishmentSupplemental2026.pdf), [SCDHEC FAQ](https://scdhec.gov/index.php/food-safety/food-safety-food-frequently-asked-questions).

### Michigan

- **Licensing:** MDARD rules, administered by local health departments.
- **Unit types:**
  - A **Transitory Food Unit (TFU, formerly STFU)** is a self-sufficient food truck that can operate statewide without returning to a base. It must carry approved SOPs and give the local health department written notice of each location before serving.
  - A **Mobile Food Establishment** must return to a licensed commissary every 24 hours.
- **Plan review:** plans must be approved before building. Detroit's reviewer has up to 30 business days and decides which license type applies.
- Sources: [MDARD food service](https://www.michigan.gov/mdard/licensing/food/service), [Detroit guide](https://detroitmi.gov/sites/detroitmi.localhost/files/forms/2018-03/How%20to%20Start%20a%20Mobile%20Food%20Business%20in%20Detroit.pdf).

### Illinois

- **Statewide:** county health departments license mobile food units under 410 ILCS 625.
- **Chicago (strictest):**
  - CDPH plan review and inspection, and a fire permit for propane, generators or hoods.
  - A licensed commissary. No food may be stored or prepared in a home.
  - A permanently installed active GPS reporting at least every 5 minutes, per a 2016 affidavit, so confirm it's current.
  - $350,000 general liability for propane or natural-gas trucks.
  - A 200-foot buffer from restaurant entrances. Sources report the Illinois Supreme Court upheld it in 2019, but this is unconfirmed from a primary source.
- Sources: [Chicago BACP](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/bacp/supp_info/mobile_food_vendorlicenses.html), [GPS affidavit](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/bacp/businesslicenseforms/businesslicenseforms/Mobile_Food_Vehicle_GPS_Affidavit_V_InPerson.pdf).

### Maryland

- **Reciprocity:** the 2017 law and COMAR 10.15.03.28 created a **mobile reciprocity license**. It covers a unit with a valid home-county license, operating within 90 miles of its base.
- **Host counties:** fees are capped at $300, and in some cases they can't require a new inspection.
- **Base of operations:** a base or commissary has been required since May 1, 2020, except for prepackaged frozen desserts.
- **Counties vary:** Queen Anne's charges $100–$250 by priority, and Garrett doesn't auto-license out-of-county units.
- Sources: [COMAR 10.15.03](https://health.maryland.gov/regs/Pages/10-15-03-.aspx), [Queen Anne's reciprocity application](https://health.maryland.gov/qahealth/environmental-health/Documents/Fillable%20Mobile%20Food%20Service%20Reciprocity%20Application%202024.pdf).

### Nevada (Las Vegas area)

- **Order of steps:** a Southern Nevada Health District permit comes first, and it requires a commissary agreement. The SNHD manual lists what the agreement must contain, including 5 business days' notice if it ends.
- **City of Las Vegas business license:**
  - the commissary agreement, and the commissary address becomes the license address;
  - liability insurance naming the city as additional insured;
  - Nevada DMV registration;
  - a fire and life safety inspection within the past 6 months.
- Sources: [City of Las Vegas](https://www.lasvegasnevada.gov/Business/Business-License/Food-Trucks), [SNHD plan review manual](https://media.southernnevadahealthdistrict.org/download/eh/2021/plan-review/resource-library/snhd-mobile-vending-plan-review-manual-for-operators.pdf).

### Alabama

- **Currently:** the health officer must approve the unit in **each county**. Approval in one county doesn't carry over (Ala. Admin. Code 420-3-22).
- **2026 change:** SB 197 is reported enacted on April 9, 2026 (Act 2026-398). A unit with a valid health certificate from its commissary's county and a valid fire certificate would be exempt from routine local re-inspections elsewhere, **starting January 1, 2027**. Local officials can still inspect during operation and close a unit for serious violations.
- **Unconfirmed:** the signing has not been confirmed from a primary source, so check the Alabama Legislature site.
- Sources: [SB197 text](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/SB197-eng.pdf), [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/AL/text/SB197/2026), [WAFF](https://www.waff.com/2026/02/27/new-bill-proposes-statewide-food-truck-inspections-alabama/).

### Minnesota

- **MDA licensing modernization:** signed May 23, 2025 and effective August 1, 2025. Mobile food units follow the Minnesota Food Code and need an MDA **Food Handler License**.
- **MDH:** still licenses some units, depending on menu and where they operate.
- **Location limit:** a mobile unit may operate no more than **21 days a year at one place**, unless it operates with a licensed permanent business.
- **Plan review:** comes before licensing.
- **Unconfirmed:** Minneapolis's local role is unconfirmed, so check with the city.
- Sources: [MDA mobile food units](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/food-feed/mobile-food-units), [MDH plan review](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/environment/food/license/mfureview.html).

## Recent changes (2025–2026)

- **Texas:** a statewide DSHS mobile food vendor license replaced local health permits on July 1, 2026 (HB 2844).
- **Alabama:** SB 197 (reported enacted in 2026) brings statewide recognition of health and fire certificates from January 1, 2027.
- **Arizona:** HB 2118, which would have removed local mobile vendor licensing, was vetoed in 2026.
- **Minnesota:** MDA's food licensing modernization took effect August 1, 2025.
- **South Carolina:** retail food and mobile unit forms are now issued by the Department of Agriculture (2024–2026 forms).
- **Georgia:** the base-county permit with authorization in other counties has applied since 2023, and the application was revised 06/2026.
- **California:** Compact Mobile Food Operations (SB 972) have been in effect since 2023.

## What agents must not do

- Don't state a fee, timeline or approval as certain. Name the agency to confirm with.
- Don't tell anyone a used unit is "permit-ready" or "approved" because a seller says so. Equipment can be *potentially suitable*, but only the health and fire authorities approve it.
- Don't give legal or tax advice beyond these summaries. Offer a team follow-up or suggest a local attorney or accountant for specifics.
